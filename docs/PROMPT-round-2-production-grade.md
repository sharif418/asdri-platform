# Round 2 — make this the institution's real platform, with nothing held back

You built this. In one autonomous run you produced a bilingual Next.js site for As-Sunnah Dawah & Research Institute with a visual identity the client liked the moment they saw it: the emerald and gold, the lattice, the Bismillah, the sense of ceremony. Other attempts were turned down for feeling plain. Yours is the one they want to keep. That is now the baseline, and nothing in this brief asks you to make it quieter.

What this brief asks is harder. Underneath the surface, this is still a prototype, and the people who will run it would discover that within a week. The content that defines the institute — the seven courses and their curricula, the thirty-three teachers, the vision, the admission process, the blog, the gallery, the statistics — lives in TypeScript files under `src/content/`; the office cannot change a teacher's designation without a developer. The database is SQLite in a file. The second language is a cookie, so Bangla and English share one URL and a search engine sees one page. `next.config.ts` carries `typescript.ignoreBuildErrors: true`, which the client explicitly forbade. There is a hard-coded fallback session secret and a default admin password in the seed. There is no Dockerfile, no compose file, no CI, no test that runs. Donations record an "intent" and nothing moves money. Admissions, the one thing the institute does on Facebook today because it has no website, has no application form. Images are generated placeholders; that is acceptable until the office uploads real ones, but only if every image on the site can be replaced from the admin.

None of that is a judgement on a first run. It is the distance between a demo and the platform the institute will be judged by for years. Your job is to cover that distance completely, and the way you will be judged is simple: a senior engineering team with months and a real budget would build this institute a platform of a certain quality. Build a better one.

## The client and the standard

As-Sunnah Dawah & Research Institute is the educational arm of As-Sunnah Foundation, the largest Islamic charity in Bangladesh. It trains young ulama and university graduates to meet the modern world's intellectual challenges from classical knowledge; qualified students study, live and eat free from the Foundation's Zakat Fund. Admissions are announced on Facebook. Donors, many abroad, want to see where Zakat goes. The three client documents are in `upload/`; the Bangla content document is the only source of Bangla copy, so copy from it and never retype it from memory. The site must be, without qualification, the best website of any dawah institute in Bangladesh, and it must stand beside the Foundation's own site, assunnahfoundation.org, as the more finished of the two. The people judging it are Bangladeshi, read on mid-range Android phones, and judge by feel.

## Use everything you have

You have a sandbox with its own disk and compute, you can run sub-agents, and you can install software. Use all of it. Your sandbox cannot run Docker, but it can run PostgreSQL 16 from portable binaries under your home directory, and an S3-compatible object store, and Redis if you decide you need one, exactly as was done for the Sunnah Life project on this same platform. Install what the architecture needs and develop against the real thing. Nothing in this project should be designed around what the sandbox finds convenient; the sandbox exists to serve the project. Where something genuinely cannot run there (Docker itself, the client's VPS, a real payment provider), make it code-complete, document how it is verified on the server, and mark it Partial until it has been.

Think as far as you can. No one is placing boundaries on the architecture, the data model, the admin, the design system or the tooling. Where this brief and the client's documents are silent, you decide, you build the better option, and you record the decision. The only thing you are not allowed to do is lower the bar to fit the time.

## What the platform must be

These are outcomes. You decide the architecture and the order, and you write both down in `docs/PLAN.md` before you write code.

**A real foundation.** PostgreSQL 16 as the database, with versioned migrations committed and applied on start, indexes where the queries need them, and a seed that loads the client's own content from the documents in both languages. Object storage through an S3-compatible adapter (local disk in development, the server's bucket in production) for every upload, with generated image sizes. A `Dockerfile` with standalone output and a `docker-compose.yml` the client can deploy on Coolify: the app, PostgreSQL, object storage, health checks, migrations on boot, secrets only from the environment. GitHub Actions that run lint, typecheck, tests and the production build on every push.

**A content system the office actually runs.** Build the admin as a product, not an afterthought: everything a staff member might ever change lives in the database and is editable there. Site identity and contact details, navigation menus, the home page as ordered, individually switchable sections, impact figures, courses with full curricula (semesters, codes, credits, specialisations, computed totals), people with roles, teams and photos, the vision and objectives, campus and facilities, alumni batches, the admission process and scholarships, FAQs, downloads, notices, blog posts with categories and authors, gallery albums, videos, research and publications, the fatwa workflow, funds and campaigns. Every visitor-facing field in Bangla and English; drafts, publishing and preview; an audit trail; roles that match the institute (administrator, editor, admissions officer, finance, fatwa board) enforced in the data layer and not only in the interface; feature flags so a module the institute is not ready for disappears from navigation, sitemap and search. The admin is in Bangla, used daily by people who are not developers, and deserves the same design care as the public site. Rich text that the office can actually write in, media they can browse, relationships they can pick from. Compare what you build against the best open-source content systems you know and ask, honestly, where yours is weaker.

**Two languages, two URLs.** Bangla is the default with no prefix; English lives under `/en`. Both server-rendered, both in the sitemap, each pointing to the other with `hreflang`. The switcher keeps the visitor on the same page. Bengali numerals for human-facing numbers in Bangla, Latin digits for codes and phone numbers, Arabic marked as Arabic.

**Admissions end to end.** Intakes per course with dates and seats; an application form (personal details, guardian, education rows, document uploads, declaration), an applicant account with the application's status, and the admissions officer's list, shortlist, exam schedule, results and CSV export.

**Donations real up to the provider.** Funds, campaigns with goal tracking, a donation flow with the anonymous option (hidden everywhere public, intact in the ledger and the receipt), a Zakat calculator that hands its result to the form, a PDF receipt and an email, and a finance ledger with filters, export and receipt re-send. The payment gateway behind an adapter with a sandbox implementation, since the client has not chosen between SSLCommerz, bKash, Nagad and aamarPay; when they do, only the adapter changes.

**Code a senior engineer is proud to hand over.** Strict TypeScript with no `any`, no `@ts-ignore`, no `eslint-disable`, `ignoreBuildErrors` gone. Files that do one thing and stay well under five hundred lines; the generated shadcn sidebar is the one tolerated exception, trimmed to what is used. Only dependencies you use. Conventional commit messages. Tests where a bug would hurt: authorisation on every admin route and API, the application and donation flows, notice status logic, numeral and currency formatting, the locale routing.

**Security that holds.** The application refuses to start in production without its secrets. Sessions httpOnly and signed, CSRF protection on every state-changing route, rate limits on public forms, the OWASP headers kept, uploads validated by type and size, payment callbacks verified by signature, and a `docs/HUMAN_STEPS.md` naming exactly which secrets and accounts only the client can create.

**Performance you have measured.** Lighthouse of 90 or better on Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO on every public page under a mid-range Android profile, with the numbers in the report. Fonts self-hosted and subset, images through `next/image`, no layout shift, motion respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Design: the identity stays, the craft rises

The client approved this direction. Build on it. What turns attractive into distinguished is craft, not more ornament: a type scale with real hierarchy where Tiro Bangla headings and Hind Siliguri text sit together with generous leading and measured line lengths; a spacing rhythm that holds from section to section; gold as the thing that catches the eye in each view rather than everywhere at once; ornament that frames content instead of competing with it; motion that happens once and means something; every page and list designed for its empty state and for the least data an office might have entered; focus states, contrast and touch targets that pass without excuses. Look at the finest institutional and scholarly sites in the world to calibrate what finished feels like, then return to this client's own material culture and make it theirs. Where you can make a screen better than this brief imagines, do it and say so.
