# Round 2 — from a beautiful prototype to the institution's real platform

You built this. In one autonomous run you produced a bilingual Next.js site for As-Sunnah Dawah & Research Institute with a visual identity the client actually liked when they saw it: the emerald and gold, the lattice, the Bismillah, the sense of ceremony. They have rejected other attempts for feeling plain or generic. Yours is the first they wanted to keep. That is the baseline now, and nothing in this brief asks you to make it quieter. It asks you to make it true.

Because underneath the surface this is still a prototype, and the people who will run it would find that out within a week. The content that defines the institute — seven courses with their curricula, thirty-three teachers, the vision, the admission process, the blog, the gallery, the statistics — lives in TypeScript files under `src/content/`. The office cannot change a teacher's designation without a developer. The database is SQLite in a file. The second language is a cookie, so Bangla and English share one URL and search engines see one page. `next.config.ts` carries `typescript.ignoreBuildErrors: true`, which the client explicitly forbade. There is a hard-coded fallback session secret and a default admin password in the seed. There is no Dockerfile, no compose file, no CI, and no test that runs. The repository still carries the scaffolding of the sandbox it was born in (`.zscripts/`, `mini-services/`, `examples/`, `tool-results/`, `upload/`, a 130 KB worklog) and ten commits whose messages are UUIDs. Several hero and campus images are AI-generated pictures of people who do not exist, on the website of a religious institution that teaches honesty. Donations record an "intent" and nothing moves money. Admissions, the one thing the institute does on Facebook today because it has no website, has no application form at all.

None of that is a criticism of a first run. It is the list of what separates a demo from the platform the institute will be judged by. Your job in this round is to close that gap completely while keeping, and deepening, the look the client approved.

## The client and the standard

As-Sunnah Dawah & Research Institute is the educational arm of As-Sunnah Foundation, the largest Islamic charity in Bangladesh. It trains young ulama and university graduates to meet the modern world's intellectual challenges from classical knowledge; qualified students study, live and eat free from the Foundation's Zakat Fund. Admissions are announced on Facebook. Donors, many abroad, want to see where Zakat goes. The three client documents are in `upload/`; the Bangla content document is the only source of Bangla copy, so copy from it and never retype it from memory. The site must be, without qualification, the best website of any dawah institute in Bangladesh, and it must stand beside the Foundation's own site, assunnahfoundation.org, as the more finished of the two. The people judging it are Bangladeshi, read on mid-range Android phones, and judge by feel.

## What must be true when you are done

Read the whole codebase first, then the three documents, then this list. These are outcomes, not steps; you decide the architecture and the order, and you write both down before you start.

**The foundation is real.** PostgreSQL 16 is the database, with Prisma migrations committed and applied, indexes where the queries need them, and a seed that loads the client's content from the documents. Your sandbox cannot run Docker, but it can run PostgreSQL: do what the Sunnah Life project did on this same platform and install the portable PostgreSQL binaries under your home directory, run it on a local port, and develop and test against it. SQLite is not a fallback; if something only works on SQLite it is not done. State in the plan exactly what runs in your sandbox and what is code-complete for the client's Coolify VPS, and never call the second kind "done".

**Everything the office might change lives in the database and is editable in the admin.** Site identity and contact details, navigation, the home page sections and their order, impact figures, courses with full curricula (semesters, course codes, credits, specialisations), people with roles and teams and photos, the vision and objectives, campus and facilities, alumni batches, the admission process and scholarships, FAQs, downloads, notices, blog posts with categories and authors, gallery albums, videos, research and publications, fatwa, funds and campaigns. Every visitor-facing field exists in Bangla and English. Media are uploaded through the admin and stored through an S3-compatible adapter (local disk in development, the VPS's object storage in production) with generated sizes. Drafts and publishing, an audit trail of who changed what, and roles that match how the institute actually works: administrator, editor, admissions officer, finance, and the fatwa board. The admin interface deserves the same care as the public site; it will be used every day, in Bangla, by people who are not developers.

**The two languages are two URLs.** Bangla is the default without a prefix; English lives under `/en`. Both are server-rendered, both in the sitemap, each pointing to the other with `hreflang`. The language switcher keeps the visitor on the same page. Bengali numerals for human-facing numbers in Bangla, Latin digits for codes and phone numbers.

**Admissions work end to end.** Intakes per course with open and close dates and seats; an application form with the common fields (personal details, guardian, education rows, document uploads, declaration); an applicant account that shows the application's status; the admissions officer's list, shortlist, exam schedule, results and CSV export. Where the documents are silent on a field, build the sensible default and record the decision.

**Donations are real up to the payment provider.** Funds, campaigns with goal tracking, a donation flow with the anonymous option, a Zakat calculator that hands its result to the form, a PDF receipt and an email, and a ledger in the admin with filters, export and receipt re-send. The payment gateway sits behind an adapter with a sandbox implementation, because the client has not chosen the provider yet (SSLCommerz, bKash, Nagad or aamarPay are the candidates). When they do, only the adapter changes.

**The code is the kind a senior engineer is proud to hand over.** Strict TypeScript with no `any`, no `@ts-ignore`, no `eslint-disable`, and `ignoreBuildErrors` gone. Files that do one thing and stay well under five hundred lines; the generated shadcn `sidebar.tsx` is the one tolerated exception, and even that should be trimmed to what is used. Dependencies you actually use, and the rest removed (check `next-auth`, `next-intl`, `@mdxeditor`, `recharts` and the others honestly). The sandbox leftovers deleted. Conventional commit messages that say what changed. A `Dockerfile` with standalone output, a `docker-compose.yml` for Coolify with the app, PostgreSQL and object storage, health checks, and migrations run on start. GitHub Actions running lint, typecheck, tests and build on every push. Tests where a bug would hurt: authorisation on every admin route, the application and donation flows, the status logic of notices, the currency and numeral formatting.

**Security is not a slogan.** No default secrets in code; the application refuses to start in production without them. Sessions httpOnly and signed, CSRF protection on every state-changing route, rate limits on the public forms, the OWASP headers you already set kept, uploads validated by type and size, access control enforced in the data layer and not only in the UI, and a `docs/HUMAN_STEPS.md` that tells the client exactly which secrets and accounts only they can create.

**Honest imagery.** Remove every AI-generated picture of a person. The institute will supply real photographs of its campus and people; until then, use designed placeholders that look intentional (the lattice, type, colour) rather than fabricated people. Decorative, non-figurative generated textures are fine.

**Performance you have measured.** Lighthouse of 90 or better on Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO for every public page on a mid-range Android profile, with the numbers in the final report. Fonts self-hosted and subset, images through `next/image`, no layout shift, motion that respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Design: keep the identity, raise the craft

The client said yes to this direction, so build on it rather than away from it. What will take it from attractive to distinguished is craft, not more ornament: a type scale with real hierarchy where Tiro Bangla headings and Hind Siliguri text sit together with generous leading and measured line lengths; a consistent spacing rhythm from section to section; gold used as the thing that catches the eye in each view rather than everywhere at once; ornament that frames content instead of competing with it; motion that happens once and means something; every list and page designed for its empty state and for the least data an office might have entered; focus states, contrast and touch targets that pass without excuses. Look at the best institutional and scholarly sites in the world to calibrate what finished feels like, then come back to this client's material culture and make it theirs. Where you can make a screen better than this brief imagines, do it, and say so in the pull request.

The one thing you must never do is call unrun work finished. A migration you did not apply, a test you did not run, a Docker build you did not execute, a page you did not open at phone width: all of these are Partial, and saying so is what makes the rest of your report trustworthy.

You have the whole picture, you have the client's approval of your visual direction, and you have no limits on how well this may be built. Build the platform the institute will still be proud of in ten years.
